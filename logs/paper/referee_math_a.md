## 2. Findings

### Finding 1 — MAJOR (gap between the paper and the main verifier; no stated theorem is false)

**Location.** Section 7.3, Remark 7.3 (`sec_verification.tex:213–226`, in particular line 223 "the main
verifier does so"); "Accelerations" (`:228–233`, "None of this is needed for correctness, and independent
implementations differ in what they use"); "Cost" (`:235–244`); Table 7, first row (`:258–262`: "Algorithm 2
with both pruning rules, every flow behind a true verdict validated literally (Remark 7.3)"); Section 7.7
(`:459–463`); `sec_intro.tex:189–193`.

**What is wrong.** The LEAF test of `verify/treecheck.cpp` is not Algorithm 2 with the two pruning rules of
Corollary 4.17. It uses a third device that the paper neither states nor proves (`treecheck.cpp:628–652`
comment, `:954–987` code): every line found strong is *deleted* — it gives up its two cells
(`deleted[l] = 1`, line 986) — and all later flows for single lines, pairs and triples are computed and
validated in the network *without* the deleted lines. `validateFlow` (`:782–812`) accordingly checks that
the deleted lines hold **no** cell (line 803), that the other non-source lines hold exactly two and that the
sources hold 2|T|+θ. Such an assignment is not a T-assignment in the sense of Definition 4.14, Proposition
4.15 (a) does not apply to it, and the counting argument of Remark 7.3 ("for every set S ⊇ U of live lines,
the lines of S then hold 2|S|+θ distinct cells of N(S)∖F") is false for sets S that contain a deleted line.
Remark 7.3 nevertheless says that the main verifier validates "these three properties".

This is not a corner case. With an instrumented copy of the verifier (`refA_treecheck_instr.patch`, counters
only) run on all 106,723 leaves: of 4,126,162 flows validated behind true verdicts, **4,002,547 (97%) are
relative to a non-empty set of deleted lines** (47 deleted lines on average). So practically every true
verdict of the main verifier with θ ≥ 1 rests on an argument that is given only in the source comment, in
`REVIEW.md` (§(b)) and in `logs/review/treecheck_audit.md` (lines 172–187), not in the paper.

Two smaller symptoms of the same discrepancy:
(a) "the main verifier computes 869,185 single-line flows" cannot be obtained from Algorithm 2, whose line 8
computes a gain for every live line of each of the 41,882 leaves with θ ≥ 1 (about four million). The number
counts only the flows for θ ≥ 2 from lines that have at least θ+2 cells outside F and from which an
augmenting path exists at all; for θ = 1 the verifier decides strength by one backward search from the
unassigned cells (`:921–967`), which is also not described (with `--paranoid`, as in the certifying run, the
flow is constructed and validated nevertheless).
(b) Remark 7.3 treats only triples that "have Gain = θ or contain a strong line"; both archive verifiers also
skip triples that contain a strong *pair*, which the remark does not cover (the same counting with U = {a,b}).

**Consequences.** Proposition 7.2 and Remark 7.3 as printed cover `verify/spotcheck.py` — I read its
`leaf_check` (lines 292–386): it is Algorithm 2 with the pair rule and the line-by-line augmentation of
Proposition 4.15 (e) — but not the program named first in Computed Fact 6.1. Theorem 1.1 is therefore still
supported by the printed argument through the second archive verifier (I did not read the leaf tests of the
two review re-expansions), and the device itself is sound and exact: proof below; brute-force test `refA_delete.py` (the deleting variant re-implemented from
the source comment agrees with Definition 4.7 on 4,200 evaluations, 724 of them false; the identity of part
(b) below holds in 9,546 random cases). But the sentences "the main verifier does so", "Algorithm 2 with both
pruning rules" and the trust statement "at least one implementation of these two algorithms" are inaccurate
for the main verifier, and a reader who audits `treecheck.cpp` against Section 7.3 will not find its
justification in the paper.

**Correction.** Add to Section 7.3 (after "Accelerations"):

```latex
\subsubsection*{Deleting strong lines}
The main verifier uses one further device, which changes the assignments on which its true
verdicts rest. As soon as a line has been found strong it is \emph{deleted}: it gives up its two
cells and is ignored in all later searches. Let $D\subseteq\live{B}$ be the set of deleted lines
and $L_D=\live{B}\setminus D$. For $T\subseteq L_D$, a \emph{$T$-assignment relative to $D$} is
a map $\alpha$ as in \cref{def:U-assignment} with values in $L_D$ such that every line of
$L_D\setminus T$ holds exactly two cells and every line of $T$ at least two; its excess is
$\sum_{l\in T}(|\alpha^{-1}(l)|-2)$. Put $\mu_D(T)=\min\{\sur_F(S):T\subseteq S\subseteq L_D\}$.

\begin{lemma}[Deleted lines]\label{lem:deleted}
Let $\theta\ge 1$, let $\mu(\{d\})\ge\theta$ for every $d\in D$, and let $T\subseteq L_D$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item If there is a $T$-assignment relative to $D$ of excess at least $\theta$, then
      $\mu(T)\ge\theta$.
\item $\min(\theta,\mu(T))=\min(\theta,\mu_D(T))$, and $\mu_D(T)$ is the number of augmentations
      from the lines of $T$ that are possible when one starts from the restriction of a pairing
      for $(B,F)$ to $L_D$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(a) Let $T\subseteq S\subseteq\live{B}$. If $S$ contains a line $d\in D$, then
$\sur_F(S)\ge\mu(\{d\})\ge\theta$. Otherwise $S\subseteq L_D$, and the lines of $S$ hold at least
$2|S|+\theta$ distinct cells of $\N(S)\setminus F$, as in the proof of \cref{prop:maxflow}\,(a).
(b) $\mu(T)$ is the smaller of $\mu_D(T)$ and the minimum of $\sur_F(S)$ over the sets
$S\supseteq T$ that meet $D$, and the latter is at least $\theta$, as in (a). The proof of
\cref{prop:maxflow} uses no property of the set $\live{B}$ of lines and applies verbatim to
$L_D$; a line of $D$ holds no cell and is never reached by an alternating path.
\end{proof}

By induction on the order of deletion, part (a) with $T=\{d\}$ shows that every deleted line is
indeed strong: when $d$ is tested, the lines deleted before it are strong. Hence single lines,
pairs and triples may be tested in the network without the deleted lines. For such a verifier the
properties to be validated behind a true verdict are: no cell is assigned twice, the deleted
lines hold no cell, every other live line outside $T$ holds exactly two cells, and the lines of
$T$ hold $2|T|+\theta$ cells together.
```

and change (i) Remark 7.3, last sentences, to "… or contains a strong line $l$ or a pair $\{a,b\}$ with
$\textsc{Gain}=\theta$, for which the same counting applies with $U=\{l\}$ or $U=\{a,b\}$. … the main
verifier does so, in the form described under `Deleting strong lines' below"; (ii) Table 7, first row, to
"Algorithm 2 with both pruning rules and deletion of strong lines (Lemma 7.x); for θ = 1 strong lines are
found by one backward search; every flow behind a true verdict validated literally"; (iii) in "Cost", say
what the 869,185 flows are (θ ≥ 2, lines with at least θ+2 cells outside F that can be augmented at all).

### Finding 2 — minor (missing step in the proof of Proposition 7.2; completeness direction only)

**Location.** `sec_verification.tex:200–202`: "The loop of lines 3–5 is the augmenting-path algorithm for the
network of Lemma 3.3 …; it ends with a pairing if one exists and fails otherwise."

**What is wrong.** Lines 3–5 are not the max-flow algorithm of Lemma 3.3: the search is restricted to
augmenting paths that start at the one line l being processed, and the algorithm stops at the first failure
while later lines still hold no cell. "No augmenting path from l" does not say that the current assignment is
maximum, so "fails otherwise" does not follow from Lemma 3.3 (or from Proposition 4.15, which starts from a
pairing). The claim is true (71 failures of this phase in `refA_sec4b`, each with the reachable set as a Hall
violator), and it only matters for false verdicts, but the argument should be printed, since the paper
otherwise avoids appeals to max-flow min-cut here.

**Correction.**
```latex
If \textsc{Augment}$(\mathit{own},\{l\})$ fails in line~\ref{ln:pair2}, let $R$ be the set of lines
that are reachable from $l$ by alternating paths. As in the proof of \cref{prop:maxflow}\,(c), every
cell of $\N(R)\setminus F$ is held by a line of $R$. At this moment every line holds at most two
cells and $l$ holds at most one; hence $|\N(R)\setminus F|\le 2|R|-1$, and there is no pairing for
$(B,F)$ by \cref{lem:hall}\,(b). If the loop ends, every live line holds exactly two cells, and
$\mathit{own}$ is a pairing.
```

### Finding 7 — editorial (Proposition 4.22 is far from sharp; optional strengthening)

**Location.** Proposition 4.22, `sec_leaf.tex:583–587`, and the sentence before it ("Sets of busy cells cannot
destroy all triangles unless they are large").

**Remark.** The statement is correct, but the bound 11 comes from a pigeonhole count that ignores the
structure. Exhaustively (`refA_sec4.py`): every set of at most **17** busy cells leaves a live triangle, and
exactly 4,096 of the 134,596 sets of 18 busy cells leave none; for busy B, L(B) contains a triangle if and only
if some face diagonal is live. A proof of the sharp bound is as short as the present one:

```latex
The two busy cells of a face diagonal have the same level $j$ and differ in exactly two
coordinates (proof of \cref{lem:busy}\,(b)); conversely any two corners of the cube
$\{j,5-j\}^3$ that differ in exactly two coordinates lie on a face diagonal
(\cref{lem:busy}\,(c), \cref{cor:collinear}). So the $12$ face diagonals of level $j$ are the
edges of two disjoint complete graphs $K_4$ on the eight corners (the corners with an even and
with an odd number of coordinates equal to $j$). A set of vertices that meets all six edges of
a $K_4$ has at least three elements. Hence a set of busy cells that meets every face diagonal
has at least six cells of each level, that is, at least $18$ cells; a set $B\subseteq\Busy$ with
$|B|\le 17$ leaves a face diagonal $d$ alive.
```
The rest of the proof is unchanged. ("at most eleven" may of course be kept; then say that the bound is not
optimal.)
